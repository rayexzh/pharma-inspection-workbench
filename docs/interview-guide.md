# Interview guide

Use this guide after you have understood the project and completed at least one exercise. Adapt every personal claim to what you actually did. All examples are fictional.

## A 60-second English introduction

> This is an AI-assisted portfolio prototype for a pharmaceutical quality-assurance support workflow. It uses six fictional inspection findings, so it contains no company or patient records. The aim is to keep each finding connected to an owner, deadline, investigation, action, internal evidence reference and review. I will show one incomplete record and explain how the workflow prevents premature closure. The checks only assess recorded information; they cannot determine whether an investigation is adequate. The app runs locally, and its history and review are demonstrations rather than validated audit records.

After doing your own review, add one true sentence such as: “I checked the official source for evidence handling and revised this example to keep an internal index rather than imply automatic submission of attachments.” Do not use that sentence until you have done the work.

## A five-minute demonstration

1. **Context:** explain the fictional organisation and the QA support task. Show the synthetic-data notice and work queue. Explain that the order is a follow-up convention, not a risk score.
2. **Source:** open one official reference. Explain what it supports and the limit of your interpretation.
3. **Record:** open F-001 from the queue and explain its missing information, including the unavailable training record. Use the evidence library to show which finding cites that record. Do not use an unrelated file as a substitute.
4. **Guard:** show why F-001 remains open. Then inspect complete, closed F-003 and clarify its action explanation; saving reopens it and invalidates approval.
5. **Follow-up:** inspect the local change log, record a new demo review, close F-003 again and export an internal report. Explain that the log has no prior-value snapshots and that the review is not authenticated.

## Questions to prepare for

| Question | What a credible answer should cover |
|---|---|
| Why build this? | A specific coordination task, the target QA role and the public sources used |
| What is the root cause? | Evidence needed, alternatives considered and what remains unknown |
| How does it assess effectiveness? | It records a plan/result; it does not independently evaluate them |
| Is it compliant? | It is an educational prototype with no claim of regulatory validation |
| What did AI do? | Explain assistance honestly, and separate it from your own reviewed changes |
| What would you improve next? | One observed weakness, acceptance criteria and feedback needed before implementation |
| What does the project prove? | A working synthetic workflow and explainable reasoning, not production experience or demand |

## CV wording after reviewing and using the project

Use only if it accurately describes your work:

> Developed and reviewed an AI-assisted, open-source pharmaceutical QA workflow prototype using synthetic inspection findings; documented source references, action tracking and internal review limits.

If you did not personally develop or review a particular part, adjust the wording. Add your repository URL only after publishing your own copy. Do not report time saved or error reduction without a defined study.

## Ask a professional for feedback

Use synthetic material and ask about past workflow experience, not confidential cases:

- Which part of inspection-response coordination usually requires the most rework?
- How are owners, commitments and internal evidence references tracked today?
- What does a reviewer need before accepting an action as complete?
- Which field or check in this demo would be misleading in your setting?
- Who decides whether a small workflow tool can be used, and what checks would they require?

Record feedback as attributed only with permission. Otherwise retain an anonymous summary. Treat comments as discovery evidence, not proof of demand or endorsement.
